# Targeted Oviposition Control for Spotted Lanternflies

**Team:** Team NYC  
**Client(s):** Cornell CALS Extension / E\&J Gallo Winery / National Grape  

## Problem statement (most important)

Vineyard managers and agricultural stakeholders are trying to prevent continued spread of Spotted Lanternflies (SLF) across vineyards and other agricultural zones, but SLF disperse unpredictably and lay egg masses on a wide range of surfaces—including vehicles—allowing eggs to be transported and establish new infestations.

Current solutions often reduce *current adult density* in locations of interest (e.g., vineyards). However, this approach does not address the next generation: SLF egg masses (each containing roughly 30–50 eggs) that remain and later hatch. The core challenge is that SLF oviposition is effectively unregulated at scale; reliance on post-oviposition elimination (scraping/destroying egg masses after they are laid) is too dispersed and large in scope to be consistently effective.

## Impact

Consolidating or redirecting SLF egg-laying into controlled zones addresses infestation at its source rather than only shifting or temporarily reducing adult presence. This would:
- Reduce the long-term compounding of SLF populations by limiting next-generation recruitment.
- Substantially reduce labor demands for scouting and scraping dispersed egg masses.
- Limit continued reinfestation and the resulting economic and ecological damage by making monitoring and destruction more targeted and reliable.

## Proposed direction(s)

### Concept A (primary): Essential-oil aerosol “No-Lay Zone” Repellent

**What it is:**  
A device that intermittently emits high concentrations of essential oils (e.g., tea tree, peppermint, lavender) prior to oviposition to create localized “no lay zones.”

**How it would be used:**
- Install multiple devices spaced roughly 10–25 ft apart from July through December near sensitive vineyard zones.
- Conduct routine adult population counts outside the ~5–10 ft radius of each device to measure adult activity and behavioral avoidance.
- Begin egg inspections in September and continue through the oviposition period.
- Routinely destroy egg masses through early spring, focusing on designated/controlled zones rather than dispersed surfaces.

**Why it’s better than the status quo:**
- Moves the intervention upstream by targeting oviposition rather than only targeting adults.
- Reduces next-generation growth compounding over time.
- Enables more concentrated monitoring and destruction instead of highly dispersed scraping efforts.

**End-of-semester proof-of-concept:**  
Develop and test an aerosol repellent module that delivers short bursts of essential oils and evaluate:
- Behavioral avoidance / reduced adult activity near treated zones,
- Evidence of reduced egg deposition in protected areas,
- Practical deployment spacing and duty-cycle for bursts.

## Key risks / unknowns

- **Environmental variability** — Wind, rain, and outdoor conditions can change aerosol transport, dilution, and deposition.  
  *Test plan:* Optimize delivery method and determine minimum effective concentration under varying conditions; evaluate drift, deposition patterns, and stability over time.

- **Population redistribution** — Redirecting SLF could unintentionally increase density in nearby untreated zones.  
  *Test plan:* Survey baseline SLF density before deployment; monitor treated vs untreated boundary zones to ensure displacement does not worsen surrounding areas.

## Questions for the client
Focus on questions they can answer from lived experience.

1. **Where are egg masses most commonly found in and around your vineyards (specific surfaces and materials)?**  
   *Decision affected:* Surface targeting and deployment location selection.

2. **When is the most critical deployment window before oviposition begins?**  
   *Decision affected:* Timing, release duration, servicing/refill interval.

3. **Are there restrictions on chemical compounds or odors near grape production areas?**  
   *Decision affected:* Repellent compound selection and allowable concentrations.
